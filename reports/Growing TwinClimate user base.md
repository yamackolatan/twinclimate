# Seed Amsterdam's runners, then prove they return

TwinClimate will not impress investors or Decathlon by chasing "as many users as possible". What will impress them is **a measured habit inside one niche**. That niche is Dutch runners and triathletes training for a race with a fixed date. They can be reached for free through clubs, race organisers and race-specific web pages. They then need a reason to come back and something worth sharing. The first job is not growth at all. **Today the app cannot prove that it has a single user**, so privacy-friendly EU analytics must go live before any promotion starts. Investors and brand partners judge early apps on retention (the share of new users still active weeks later) and on how often people use them, far more than on sign-up totals. Health and fitness apps typically keep only **3–4% of users at day 30**, while weather apps see **32–44% of their monthly users on a typical day**. A weather-driven training tool that clearly beats the fitness baseline therefore has a credible story. The competition supports this plan. Dutch weather is owned by Buienradar and Infoplaza (about 10 and 16 million people a month). "What to wear" tools are plentiful but small. And no tool was found that matches a race's historical climate to nearby training places and times. A realistic first milestone, around February 2027, is **300–500 weekly active users in the Netherlands**, with a quarter of each week's newcomers still active four weeks later and 5–7% weekly growth coming mostly from clubs and word of mouth. That is enough to open a commercial conversation with Decathlon Netherlands, but not enough on its own for an investment round. The app should stay a web app for now, installable from the browser, with a proper landing page on its own domain. App-store versions come next. Google Play in particular requires a real Android phone and 12 testers for 14 days. The main risks: big weather apps or Decathlon add kit advice themselves, Strava's 2024–2026 rules make integrations slow and fragile, and one founder runs out of hours or fails partner programmes that require two founders.

*How solid is the evidence? The five research notes behind this report were compiled on 23 October 2026. The researchers could only read search-result extracts, because direct page reads were blocked. Many benchmarks (retention, push notifications, widgets, ad costs) come from vendors who sell those services, and they often contradict each other. Treat single numbers as rough ranges, and re-check any figure before it goes into an investor deck. The effort, impact and milestone figures in the plan are judgement, not data. Facts about the app itself come from the developer.*

## The winners grew from one niche and a shareable output, not from ads

Strava did not start as an app for everyone. Its founders picked "passionate road cyclists" as their first market, to build fierce loyalty before adding other sports ([Stanford STVP](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/strava-finds-its-niche-transcript.pdf)). They gave **ten Garmin units** to keen riders on the US East and West Coasts and ran coast-versus-coast competitions during the 2008 Tour de France ([Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook)). Segments and leaderboards were built because riders said climbs mattered most and wanted to compare themselves with friends on the same stretch of road ([Terra podcast with Mark Gainey](https://landing-cf.tryterra.co/podcast/podcast-with-mark-gainey-co-founder-of-strava-97bfd4bd85e9)). After that, group rides did the marketing. Scale came slowly: **more than 180 million users by December 2025** and about 200 million by August 2026, some 16 years after launch ([Strava via Adnkronos](https://www.adnkronos.com/immediapress/eng/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-revealing-that-doomscrolling-is-out-movement-is-in_iPmltuOBI6UULjUCMcmDr); [Forge Global](https://forgeglobal.com/insights/strava-upcoming-ipo-news/)).

Runna is the closest model for a product built around a race goal. It began in 2021 as hand-made PDF training plans for one friend's first marathon ([Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/)). Its founders name organic growth, especially referrals, as a lead channel, alongside creators and partnerships. One commentator credits its choice of **relatable creators over elite athletes**, whose posts follow the plan week by week to race day ([Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/runna-choosing-relatability-over-celebrities)). Runna also partnered with the New York City Marathon and parkrun UK. It reported about **30% month-on-month growth** after launching its subscription in 2022, a self-reported figure ([Fifty.io](https://fifty.io/blog/running-the-distance-how-runna-and-russ-cook-are-changing-the-game); [Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/)). Strava bought it in April 2025 ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/strava-acquires-runna-amid-spike-in-race-day-goals/)).

Relive, from Rotterdam, is the best Dutch example of a tiny team going big. It turned GPS rides from Strava and Garmin into 3D videos. In its first year it reached almost **500,000 sign-ups and about 100,000 videos a day** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/relive)). A co-founder said **one in three viewed videos gets shared**, calling the product "a marketing tool for its users" ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/relive-app-3d-video-news/)). Relive later reported more than 20 million users on reported funding of only about €1.2 million ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/relive-acquires-cycling-training-app-join/); [StartupBlink](https://startupblink.com/startup/relive--2)). Windy, the Czech weather map, grew to tens of millions of monthly users without ads. It started with a founder fixing his own kite-trip forecasts, though he was already wealthy ([CzechCrunch](https://cc.cz/z-50-na-400-milionu-za-pet-let-aplikace-na-pocasi-windy-iva-lukacovice-dal-roste-a-naramne-vydelava/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-02-06/windy-com-is-the-new-favorite-website-for-the-climate-obsessed)).

The pattern repeats: **one tight community, an output people show off or compete on, and spread through groups that already exist** such as group rides, run clubs and Strava feeds. None of these apps grew first through paid ads. Two warnings come with the pattern. First, it takes years. komoot needed about 12 years after its public launch to reach 45 million users before Bending Spoons bought it in March 2025 ([Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/bending-spoons-acquires-komoot)). Second, platforms change their terms. komoot's February 2025 paywall for new users was mocked as "we don't want any new customers" ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/03/11/komoot-confirms-we-dont-want-any-new-customers-in-ridiculous-pricing-move/)), and Strava's 2024 rules hurt apps built on its data (see below). TwinClimate's use of open, keyless Open-Meteo data avoids one such dependency.

### Dutch weather is taken; athlete advice and race matching are open

General weather in the Netherlands is a closed market. RTL's Buienradar reaches about **10 million people a month** across TV, web and app ([Ad Alliance](https://adalliance.nl/wp-content/uploads/Factsheet-Buienradar.pdf)). Infoplaza's Weeronline, Weerplaza and Buienalarm together reach about **16 million a month**. Buienalarm passed 5 million active users in June 2025, even in an unusually dry half-year ([Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/buienalarm-passeert-5-miljoen-gebruikers-extreem-droog-jaar)). These brands own distribution through TV, press and search. Dutch people also pay little: Buienradar's ad-free upgrade cost about €3.49 a year ([AndroidWorld](https://androidworld.nl/nieuws/buienradar-wordt-beter-in-het-buitenland/)). RTL has cut outside developers off its data before ([iCulture](https://www.iculture.nl/apps/regen-verdwijnt-uit-app-store-vanwege-nieuw-beleid-buienradar/)). Competing on forecasts is pointless. TwinClimate should present itself as the athlete layer on top: "what do I wear at 08:00 in the Amsterdamse Bos, and why?"

"What to wear" tools exist in large numbers, but none has grown big. Most are one-developer iPhone apps or simple web calculators with a hot/cold slider ([NorthLab Apps](https://northlabapps.com/blog/what-to-wear-running.md)). A much-loved cycling route-weather app, Epic Ride Weather, had only **739 US App Store ratings** after about ten years ([AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/1159726753)). Brands see the idea as a way to sell kit. Assos launched an app that recommends its own clothing from the forecast ([BikeBiz](https://bikebiz.com/cycling-apparel-brand-assos-launches-its-first-mobile-app/)), and On, Satisfy and Bandit reportedly built whatdoiwear.run ([ProductCool](https://www.productcool.com/product/whatdoiwear-run)). One iPhone app, Do I Need A Coat?, already learns from "too cold / too warm" feedback ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6751337989)), so the learning loop alone is not unique. As far as their descriptions show, none combines TwinClimate's exact-spot, session-hour forecast, a reason for every item across five body parts, a learned profile per sport, a packing checklist and race-climate matching.

Race matching looks like an empty niche. The research found **no tool that takes a target race's historical race-hour climate and finds nearby places and times that match it**. Pieces exist. FindMyMarathon publishes per-race weather averages over 12–18 years ([FindMyMarathon](https://www.findmymarathon.com/weather-detail.php?zname=Miami+Marathon)). Weather Spark shows typical weather for 145,449 places ([Travel Massive](https://travelmassive.com/posts/weather-spark-844438344)). Garmin's heat-acclimation score simply counts outdoor activities above 22 °C ([Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-3B607AEB-9EB5-4BF3-A38D-1F9926BB9F65/EN-US/GUID-70386BCC-5682-4C5C-9A87-C32AF9B6473B.html)). Triathletes on forums still pull averages from general weather sites by hand ([Slowtwitch](https://forum.slowtwitch.com/forum/Slowtwitch_Forums_C1/Triathlon_Forum_F1/coldest_ironman%3F_P473060/)). The niche is small, mostly marathoners and triathletes with a far-away race. But it is the sharpest thing to lead with in pitches and press.

| Group | Examples | Scale | Lesson for TwinClimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social fitness platforms | Strava, komoot, Relive | 20–200 million users, after 10–16 years | Copy the niche-first, shareable-output pattern; don't depend on their data |
| Race-goal coaching | Runna, Start 2 Run | Hundreds of thousands to millions | A race date creates months of committed use; organisers and creators drive growth |
| Dutch weather brands | Buienradar, Weeronline, Buienalarm | 10–16 million a month | Don't compete on forecasts; be the athlete layer |
| What-to-wear tools | dressmyrun, Do I Need A Coat?, Epic Ride Weather, Assos, whatdoiwear.run | Small; no public user numbers | The need is real, but growth needs a host: clubs, search or a brand |
| Race-climate pieces | FindMyMarathon, Weather Spark, Garmin acclimation | Pieces only | No direct competitor; the strongest story |

## About 700 clubs and a few race organisers are the free way in

The Netherlands has plenty of athletes, but how many depends on the definition. NOC*NSF counted **924,000 runners in 2024** ([Hardlooptrainers Nederland](https://www.hardlooptrainersnederland.nl/artikel/11563/hardlopen-groeit.html)). The Rotterdam Marathon's 2026 press kit says 3.9 million people run or jog and 1.6 million run at least weekly ([NN Marathon Rotterdam](https://nnmarathonrotterdam.nl/wp-content/uploads/sites/52/2026/04/Persmap-NN-Marathon-Rotterdam-2026-compressed.pdf)). Ipsos counted about **1.6 million "sporty cyclists"**, 1.3 million of them on road bikes ([NTFU](https://stakeholders.ntfu.nl/api/download/a8e74812-b3d9-f011-b202-005056a6b14d/modules/148)). The organised core is much smaller and easier to reach. It includes **116,071 athletics club members in 318 clubs** ([Atletiekunie](https://atletiekunie-global.b-cdn.net/production/atletiekunie/De-Atletiekunie/Jaarplannen-eeen-Jaarverslagen/Statistisch-Jaarverslag/Statistisch-Jaarverslag-2024.pdf?v=1745501261)), about 80 affiliated running groups or *loopgroepen* ([Atletiekunie](https://www.atletiekunie.nl/de-atletiekunie/wie-zijn-wij/)), and about 75,000 NTFU cycling members ([NTFU](https://www.ntfu.nl/api/download/fb85d1b6-7039-f111-b206-005056a6b14d/modules/1571)). Triathlon adds **139 clubs, 92 event organisers, about 11,000 members and about 27,000 day-licence holders** a year ([NTB](https://www.triathlonbond.nl/)). Together that is a list of roughly 700 or more organised groups that one person can work through in a few months (our estimate). Big events show how committed these people are. The TCS Amsterdam Marathon weekend drew 60,000+ runners in 2025 ([Rodi](https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/nieuws/468399/50e-editie-marathon-van-amsterdam-geslaagd-gezellig-en-een-tikkeltje-chaotisch)), and the 2026 race sold out within a week ([Rodi](https://www.rodi.nl/amsterdam-nieuw-west/sport/479704/tcs-amsterdam-marathon-2026-binnen-week-uitverkocht)). Rotterdam had about 18,000 starters in 2026, and about 32,000 people missed out in its lottery ([NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2609845)). People who enter that far ahead train for a known date. That is exactly TwinClimate's race-fingerprint user.

Clubs and trainers are the best free channel. One trainer posts in a WhatsApp group of 20–100 runners, so 50 trainers could plausibly reach 1,000–5,000 people (our estimate). Runna helped its US ambassadors set up run clubs ([Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/runna-choosing-relatability-over-celebrities)). Young runners join clubs to meet people: a LADbible Group study found **72% of Gen Z join run clubs for social reasons and 65% post about their runs** ([National Running Show](https://nationalrunningshow.com/?p=24621)). The offer to a trainer should be concrete: a free "club session forecast" link to paste into the group chat before Saturday's session, showing the hourly weather at the meeting spot and what to wear.

Race organisers have a safety reason to care. At the hot 2024 Dam tot Damloop, **ambulances were called about 50 times and about 4,500 runners in the last waves were not allowed to start** ([NH Nieuws](https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/340882/ambulance-ruim-50-keer-ingezet-bij-dam-tot-damloop)). For 2025 the organiser moved the starts an hour earlier ([nieuws.nl](https://nieuws.nl/sport/start-dam-tot-damloop-vervroegd-na-overbelasting-hulpdiensten)). At Rotterdam in 2024, **77 runners were treated for overheating**. An Erasmus MC race doctor advised getting used to race conditions for two weeks ([NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2025/04/11/doctor-warns-rotterdam-marathon-runners-watch-signs-heat-stress)), which is exactly what TwinClimate's race matching helps with. Rotterdam's own guidance covers clothing only as shoes and socks ([NN Marathon Rotterdam](https://nnmarathonrotterdam.nl/en/run-responsibly/)). So a free, co-branded "race-day weather and what to wear" page for each race's pre-race email is a credible pitch. Le Champion runs both Amsterdam and Dam tot Dam, about 140,000 participations between them. One yes there would be huge, but expect a long sales cycle. Mid-size runs and triathlon organisers are easier first targets. Belgium shows how powerful the organiser route can be: the organiser Golazo's Start 2 Run app gains about **17,500 new users a month** ([Golazo](https://www.golazo.com/cases/annually-1-3-million-workouts-are-completed-with-the-start-2-run-app-every-month-17500-new-start-2-runners-join-in/)). No Dutch organiser was found sending kit advice today, so their interest is an inference.

Media and creators can amplify, but they need a story. Hardloopnieuws gets about half its traffic from Google ([Hardloopnieuws](https://www.hardloopnieuws.nl/colofon/onze-bereikcijfers)), and Weeronline already writes race-day weather pieces ([Weeronline](https://www.weeronline.nl/nieuws/10-4-2024-zo-warm-wordt-het-tijdens-de-marathon-van-Rotterdam)). A data story such as "ten years of Amsterdam Marathon race-morning weather" or "where near Amsterdam you can train in Kona-like heat" has a better chance than a product pitch. NOS credits "runfluencers" with part of the demand for Dutch marathons ([NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2609845)). Runna's lesson suggests a few relatable small creators following one race block will beat celebrities. Search engine optimisation (SEO, meaning being found on Google) is slow but builds over time. English searches like "what to wear running" are owned by Nike, REI and other big names ([Nike](https://www.nike.com/a/what-to-wear-by-temperature); [REI](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/running-clothing.html)). TwinClimate should target longer Dutch queries and race-specific pages instead, copying the FindMyMarathon model of one public page per race.

Launch websites give short spikes, not lasting growth. Coming third of the day on Product Hunt brought one product **1,305 visitors and 210 sign-ups**, and traffic fell sharply the next day ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/indiemerger/we-placed-3rd-on-product-hunt-and-earned-543)). A Hacker News front page brought another about 500 visitors ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/front-page-of-hn-the-full-postmortem-traffic-lessons-surprises-cbe9e0a7f6)). Most of those visitors are tech people outside the Netherlands. Reddit's r/running bans self-promotion outside a weekly thread ([r/running mirror](https://reddit.sudovanilla.org/r/running/comments/1h3532h/social_saturday)). Paid ads are affordable for testing but not for growth. A Dutch Facebook or Instagram ad click costs a median of about **€0.73**, and fitness clicks about €0.42 ([Superads](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpc-cost-per-click/netherlands); [Searchlab](https://searchlab.nl/statistieken/facebook-ads-statistieken-2026)). So €500 buys roughly 640–1,190 visits. Benchmarks for the cost per app install (CPI) in health and fitness range from about $1.50 to $6 ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/ads/cpi/research/cost-per-install/); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/customer-acquisition-cost/)). A €50–200 test to find the best message makes sense. Investors value organic growth more than bought users anyway.

## Retention decides the pitch, and today nothing brings users back

**Retention** is the share of new users who still use an app some time later. "Day-30 retention" (D30) is the share who are active on day 30 after their first visit. **DAU/MAU** divides daily active users by monthly active users, which tells you what share of a month's users show up on a typical day. **WAU/MAU** does the same for a week. On these measures, health and fitness apps leak badly. Compilations of AppsFlyer and Adjust data put them at about **20–24% on day 1, about 8% on day 7 and 3–4% on day 30** ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/mobile-app-retention); [Adjust](https://www.adjust.com/blog/what-makes-a-good-retention-rate/)). Weather apps are among the stickiest everyday tools. In the US in June 2024, **AccuWeather's DAU/MAU was 44% and The Weather Channel's 40%**, and these ratios rise in dramatic weather and fall in calm spells ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/inclement-weather-patterns-impact-weather-app-user-stickiness)). Benchmark pages disagree a lot: fitness D30 is quoted anywhere from 3% to 12%, and some pages relabel old data as new. No single figure should go into a pitch without its source. Most amateur athletes train a few times a week, not daily, so weekly measures are a fairer yardstick for TwinClimate than daily ones ([PM Toolkit](https://pmtoolkit.ai/how-to/calculate-dau-mau)).

TwinClimate starts with a built-in advantage. Workout apps make users train for weeks before they see results ([Snoopr](https://www.snoopr.co/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks-2026-what-good-looks-like-for-fitness-ecommerce-gaming-and-more)). TwinClimate gives a useful answer, the kit list, within a minute and without an account. Letting people get value before asking them to sign up is standard best practice. One vendor test found that delaying account creation raised trial starts by 20%, although that figure is unverified ([Purchasely](https://www.purchasely.com/blog/app-onboarding-process)). First impressions matter most, because people drop apps fastest right after first use. A 2024 review of 18 studies found that a median of **70% of health-app users stop within 100 days** ([JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897)). So the first screen should open on a ready-made answer (nearest favourite place, tomorrow at 07:00, the user's sport) rather than an empty form.

The weakness is just as clear: **nothing brings a user back**. There are no notifications, no home-screen widget and no saved weekly schedule, and users must re-enter their session each time. Peer-reviewed research names loss of motivation, loss of interest and trying other apps as the main reasons people drop health apps ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8872344/)). Industry surveys add manual data entry and generic content. For TwinClimate the specific threats are users thinking "my phone's weather app is good enough" on mild days, retyping the session every time, and an off-season when race matching has nothing to do. All data also lives in the browser. A user who clears it or switches phone loses their comfort profile, and once analytics exist they would be counted as a new user.

The best-evidenced way to bring users back is the notification. Airship's 2023 benchmark found that users who received any push message in their first 90 days had about **three times higher retention** than those who received none ([Airship](https://grow.urbanairship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2023-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-en.pdf)). That is a correlation, not a controlled test, and later coverage reports a smaller effect. On the web it is harder. iPhones allow web push only after the user adds the web app to the Home Screen and then taps to subscribe ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)). Apple planned to remove Home Screen web apps in the EU in 2024 but reversed that decision ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/03/02/apple_reverses_pwa_decision/)). Widgets have only weak evidence. The Gratitude journaling app reported 25% higher retention among widget users, with no comparison group ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/05/how-gratitude-widgets-boosted-user-retention-25-percent.html)). Streaks have the best experiment: Duolingo reported a 3.3% relative gain in day-14 retention ([Apsteq](https://apsteq.com/blog/duolingo-streak-teardown/)). But a daily streak clashes with sensible training, which includes rest days. A weekly count of planned sessions fits better.

There are two practical layers. The cheap layer can be done now. Save each user's regular sessions so the app opens on "your next session", and offer a calendar file so sessions appear in the user's own calendar. Neither needs a server, though no retention evidence exists for calendar export. The stronger layer is an evening-before or morning-of alert, such as "Tomorrow 07:00 Vondelpark: feels like 6°, wet — gloves and a rain shell". That one is the founder's decision. He cancelled the earlier packing reminder (F5), and the brief puts "proactive plan-aware notifications" in V3. On the web it would also need a small server to send the messages, which conflicts with "no backend yet". Native apps make it easier. The learned comfort profile is the other retention asset. A user who has taught the app "I run warm above 15 °C" loses that by switching apps. The effect is plausible but unmeasured.

## Session links and kit cards can copy Relive's loop without a server

The apps that spread fastest gave users something to show off. Spotify Wrapped 2025 reached **200 million users on its first day** and was reportedly shared more than 500 million times ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day); [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/music/news/spotify-wrapped-breaks-own-record-250-million-engagements-1236603493/)). Strava made its Year in Sport recap free in 2024, with video and still versions designed for sharing ([Strava Community Hub](https://communityhub.strava.com/what-s-new-10/your-2024-year-in-sport-is-here-8084)). Its own data show that group activities with more than 10 people are 40% longer than solo ones ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-2024-year-in-sport-report/)). Roast My Strava passed 100,000 users on the back of an Instagram trend in December 2025 ([Apps for Strava](https://appsforstrava.com/blog/how-strava-apps-get-users)). In TwinClimate's own niche, What to Wear Cycling added a "Tell the Peloton" button that shares the kit list with a riding group ([London Cyclist](https://londoncyclist.co.uk/new-app-what-to-wear)). Klimat writes the weather into each Strava activity, where followers see it, and had done so for **80 million activities** by February 2026 ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/02/03/klimat-strava-weather-review/)). No evidence was found that "invite a friend, get a reward" schemes work for free fitness tools.

Four shareable outputs fit TwinClimate, and the first three need no accounts and no server. The first is a **"share this session" link**. It carries the place, date, time and sport in the web address. A friend who opens it sees the hourly forecast and *their own* kit list, worked out from their own comfort profile. That makes it a natural loop: to see what *they* should wear, the friend has to open TwinClimate. The second is a **kit card** image ("Saturday 08:00 Vondelpark · feels like 6°, 9° in the sun · long tights, gloves, light jacket · made with TwinClimate") sized for run-club WhatsApp groups and Instagram Stories. The third is a **race-day climate card** ("Ironman Kalmar, 29 Aug, 07:00–16:00: typically 17–20°, humid, 30% of race days wet · my closest training match near Amsterdam: 82%"). It turns the app's most distinctive result into something triathletes want to post. The fourth, a year-end **"your climate year" recap** (coldest session, most-used kit item, what the app learned), fits December, when Strava's Year in Sport is in the news. Only users with months of history on their device would have one, though. The design rule from Relive and Wrapped is that the card shows off the user, not the app, and carries the link quietly.

A "TwinClimate line" in the user's own Strava activities ("Felt 7°, wind 18 km/h — long sleeve + gloves, felt just right · twinclimate.app") would copy Klimat and suits the product. But it is slow and fragile. Since 11 November 2024, an app may show a user's Strava data only to that same user ([Strava Support](https://support.strava.com/en-us/articles/15401608-api-agreement-update-how-data-appears-on-3rd-party-apps)). The agreement bans using Strava data to train AI or machine-learning models ([Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-api-changes/)). New apps start with **one athlete**, can raise that to 10 themselves, and need Strava's review to go beyond 10 ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/strava-api-changes-2026/)). Since June 2026 the developer of a standard app must hold a paid Strava subscription, reported at **$11.99 a month** ([Apps for Strava](https://appsforstrava.com/blog/strava-developer-program-changes-2026); [AI Chat Daily](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/strava-puts-api-behind-11-99-paywall-blaming)). Strava's club data features were removed in September 2026 ([Strava changelog](https://developers.strava.com/docs/changelog/)). Strava also cut Relive off in 2019 ([DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2019/07/strava-platform-dispute.html)) and launched its own weather feature in 2020 ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2020/04/03/new-strava-weather-service-vs-klimat-strava-add-in/)). A Strava connection needs a server to handle sign-in safely, which the brief does not yet allow, and Strava and Garmin are V3 items. No source confirms or forbids writing to activity descriptions under the 2026 terms, so the founder should ask Strava's developer support before anything is built. Garmin is off the table for now: it stopped accepting new applications to its Connect developer programme in spring 2026 ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/09/14/garmin-developer-api-access-paused/)).

## Stay on the web now; the app stores need a phone and twelve testers

A **PWA** (progressive web app) is a website that can be installed on a phone's home screen and then opens like an app. TwinClimate is almost there. Its web manifest (the small file describing the app to browsers) already has the name, icons, start address and app-style display that Chrome requires before offering installation ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/install-criteria)). Since iOS 26, any site added to the iPhone Home Screen opens as a web app by default ([Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/?p=49472)). iPhone users still have to find Share → "Add to Home Screen" themselves. The AI assistant can add a visible "Install app" button for Android and desktop Chrome, plus a short hint for iPhones. One fix is urgent. The manifest still says "Compare city climates and find your climate twin", and the page description says "Compare the climates of two cities…". That is what search engines and install dialogs show today. For growth driven by WhatsApp groups, a link that opens instantly with no store visit is an advantage, not a compromise.

The web app cannot win search traffic on its own. Flutter web draws the app onto a canvas, so search engines see little readable text ([pub.dev flutter_prerender](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_prerender)). Advice from the Flutter community in 2026 is to keep Flutter for the app and build marketing pages as plain HTML ([The Flutter Kit](https://theflutterk.it.com/blog/flutter-web-2026-production-ready)). Flutter web also downloads about **1.5 MB** of drawing engine before showing anything ([Flutter blog](https://flutter.dev/blog/best-practices-for-optimizing-flutter-web-loading-speed)), which matters on mobile data. TwinClimate therefore needs a separate, fast, simple landing page plus the per-race pages described earlier, in Dutch and English. They should sit on a custom domain. A .app domain costs roughly **$8–14 a year** and works with GitHub Pages, including free HTTPS ([domainoffer.net](https://domainoffer.net/tld/app/cloudflare); [GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/securing-your-github-pages-site-with-https)). A github.io address under a personal username looks unfinished to Decathlon, and every shared link breaks if hosting ever moves. The same site can host the privacy policy and support page that both app stores require.

App stores add discovery and stronger tools for bringing users back. **ASO** (app store optimisation) means writing the store listing so the app turns up in store search. About 65% of App Store downloads reportedly follow a search ([Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/app-store-optimization-statistics)). The hurdles are practical, not technical. On **Google Play**, a new personal developer account must verify a **real Android phone** (Android 10 or newer, not modified) through the Play Console app ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14316361)). It must also run a closed test with **at least 12 testers for 14 days in a row** before going public ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)). An organisation account skips the tester rule but needs a registered company and a D-U-N-S business number. The one-off fee is $25 (not re-checked in this research). **Apple** costs **$99 a year**, needs an Apple Account with two-step login, and shows the founder's legal name as the seller ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)). Apple reviews at least half of submissions within 24 hours ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). No Mac is needed. Codemagic builds and signs iPhone apps in the cloud, with **500 free build minutes a month** ([Codemagic](https://docs.codemagic.io/billing/pricing/)). It does recommend uploading the very first version by hand ([Codemagic](https://docs.codemagic.io/flutter-publishing/publishing-to-app-store/)), which should be checked with Codemagic's support. **Which store comes first depends on the phone in the founder's pocket.** With an Android phone, Google Play is within reach, and the 12-tester test can double as a club pilot. With only an iPhone, Apple is easier, and he can test builds himself through Apple's TestFlight app. With neither, he must borrow one. The research could not confirm whether Apple sign-up works fully without an Apple device.

### Dutch buys trust more than understanding

The Dutch read English better than anyone else: the Netherlands ranked **first of 123 countries** in the 2025 EF English Proficiency Index ([EF EPI](https://www.ef.edu/epi/)). Language is still about trust. In a 2020 survey of 8,709 consumers in 29 countries, **76% preferred product information in their own language and 40% never bought from sites in another language** ([CSA Research](https://csa-research.com/blogs-events/csa-in-the-media/press-releases/consumers-prefer-their-own-language)). That is a global average and is probably much lower in the Netherlands. The only measured app result is old. iPhone apps that added a local language saw downloads rise 128% in the first week (Distimo, 2012), best treated as an upper limit ([OneSky](https://www.oneskyapp.com/blog/localization-increases-downloads-by-128-on-average-for-iphone-apps/)). The sensible order runs like this. First, Dutch versions of the landing page, race pages and store listing, which are cheap and target Dutch search terms. Second, Dutch text inside the app, ideally before the store launch. Third, German: Germany is the biggest EU athlete market, and 57% of Germans buy only on sites in their own language ([CSA Research](https://csa-research.com/blogs-events/csa-in-the-media/press-releases/consumers-prefer-their-own-language)). After that come French, Spanish and Italian, where English is much weaker; France ranks 38th and Italy 59th ([EF France](https://www.ef.com/assetscdn/WIBIwq6RdJvcD9bc8RMd/cefcom-epi-site/fact-sheets/2025/ef-epi-fact-sheet-france-french.pdf); [EF Italy](https://www.ef.com/assetscdn/WIBIwq6RdJvcD9bc8RMd/cefcom-epi-site/fact-sheets/2025/ef-epi-fact-sheet-italy-english.pdf)).

Most of the work is moving about 700 English sentences into translation files. Many of them are assembled by the kit, matching and fingerprint logic. English dates such as "Sat 24 Oct" are also typed into the code by hand and must switch to Dutch formats. Flutter's standard tools handle the rest ([Flutter docs](https://docs.flutter.dev/ui/internationalization)). The AI assistant can do the extraction and a first translation in a few sessions. A native Dutch-speaking athlete should then check kit words such as "gilet" and "arm warmers". The brief lists only Turkish localisation (V2), so Dutch is a new decision for the founder. Turkish matters less for growth in the Netherlands. The European Accessibility Act, in force since June 2025, probably does not apply: it covers listed services such as e-commerce and banking, and microenterprises with fewer than 10 staff and under €2 million turnover are exempt from its service rules ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/882/article/4); [Complianz](https://complianz.io/definition/what-is-the-european-accessibility-act/)). Basic accessibility labels are still worth adding, because the same work helps screen readers and search engines read the app.

## Investors and Decathlon will ask for cohorts, not totals

Every investor framework the research found rewards habit and organic growth over headline totals. The venture firm a16z rates DAU/MAU of **25% as OK, 40% as good and 50%+ as great**. It rates monthly user growth at the early "seed" stage at 20% OK, 35% good and 50% great, "ideally mostly organic" ([a16z](https://a16z.com/do-you-have-lightning-in-a-bottle-how-to-benchmark-your-social-app/)). Paul Graham's Y Combinator rule of thumb is **5–7% growth a week** in active users for a company that does not yet charge ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/12/18/growth-as-a-false-signal-in-y-combinator-startups/?ncid=rss)). Growth experts surveyed by Lenny Rachitsky call about 25–30% of users still active after six months "good" for consumer apps ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29); [Founders Fuel](https://foundersfuel.beehiiv.com/p/good-great-retention)). Sifted's ranking of European consumer start-ups judges "reach, habit and buzz" from store ratings, web traffic, search interest and social mentions ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/rankings/sifted-consumer-100-2026)). A **cohort** is the group of users who started in the same week. A cohort chart shows what share of each group is still active in later weeks, and the key signal is whether that curve flattens out instead of sliding to zero. At the earliest "pre-seed" stage, European investors back the founder, the market and conviction more than traction ([Pitchwise](https://www.pitchwise.se/blog/top-pre-seed-investors-in-europe-2026)). In practice, a solo non-technical founder will be judged hard on his team.

The metric set below fits a weekly training tool. The targets are our judgement, built from the benchmarks above. No source sets thresholds for an app like this.

| Metric | Plain meaning | Why it persuades | First target (judgement) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly active users (WAU) | People who looked up at least one session that week | An honest, defined headline number | 300–500 in the Netherlands by end of February 2027 |
| Week-4 cohort retention | Share of a week's newcomers still active four weeks later | The first chart investors look at | 25% or more, flattening by week 8 |
| WAU/MAU | Share of the month's users who are active in a given week | Strength of the habit | 50% or more |
| Weekly growth, split by source | Growth from clubs, shared links and search versus ads | "Mostly organic" signals quality | 5–7% a week for 8 weeks; at least 70% unpaid |
| Kit lists per active user per week | Sessions planned | Real use, not just visits | 2 or more |
| Feedback-card response rate | Share of finished sessions that get rated | Proves the learning loop works | 30% or more |
| Shop funnel | Kit lists → shop sheet opened → click to decathlon.nl; "already have it" rate | Buying intent tied to the weather | Report honestly; no benchmark exists |
| Shares and opened links | Cards shared, and links opened by new people | The viral loop | Track from launch |

### Cookieless EU analytics can count users without a cookie banner

Today TwinClimate counts nothing centrally. The Partner demo screen counts only on each person's own device. Dutch law (Telecommunicatiewet article 11.7a) requires consent before storing or reading anything on a user's device. There are exceptions for strictly necessary storage and for analytics with "no or only minor" privacy impact, provided any outside processor signs a processing agreement and does not use the data for itself ([ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/blog/tracking-cookies-zonder-geldige-toestemming-autoriteit-persoonsgegevens-stuurt-waarschuwingsbrieven); [Kamerstuk 24095-344](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/kst-24095-344.pdf)). Standard Google Analytics 4 is widely expected to need consent in the Netherlands ([privacy-web.nl](https://privacy-web.nl/en/nieuws/google-analytics-cookies-plaatsen-zonder-toestemming-mag-dat/)). The Dutch privacy regulator (AP) has been enforcing cookie-banner rules and sent about 50 warning letters in April 2025 ([ICTRecht](https://www.ictrecht.nl/blog/tracking-cookies-zonder-geldige-toestemming-autoriteit-persoonsgegevens-stuurt-waarschuwingsbrieven)). European regulators also say "cookieless" does not automatically mean exempt, because the rule covers fingerprinting and other ways of reading a device ([EDPB](https://edpb.europa.eu:443/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)). France's regulator CNIL publishes a practical test for exempt visitor counting. Users must be informed and able to object, the data may be used only for measurement, IP addresses are shortened, and data is kept for at most 13 months ([CNIL](https://cnil.fr/en/node/677)). That is a sensible model for the Netherlands too. This is not legal advice. A short check with a Dutch privacy adviser before the investor pitch is worth considering.

|---|---|---|---|
| PostHog Cloud EU | Frankfurt ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/posthog-cloud-eu)) | First 1 million events a month free ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/pricing)) | Official Flutter kit, including web ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/docs/web-analytics/installation/flutter)); funnels and cohorts built in |
| Umami Cloud | Not confirmed | Free "Hobby" plan, 100,000 events a month ([Cloudzy](https://cloudzy.com/blog/umami-review/)) | No Flutter kit; a small script in the web page |
| Plausible | Germany, no cookies, daily-changing hashes ([european-alternatives.eu](https://european-alternatives.eu/product/plausible.io)) | From about $9 a month ([apiscout](https://apiscout.dev/blog/plausible-vs-umami-vs-fathom-analytics-2026)) | Weak community Flutter kit ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/documentation/plausible)) |
| Simple Analytics | Amsterdam-based company | Free plan with a required badge ([freetier.co](https://freetier.co/directory/products/simple-analytics)) | A small script in the web page |
| Google Analytics 4 / Firebase | Google | Free | Needs a consent banner ([Google](https://developers.google.com/admob/flutter/privacy/consent-mode?hl=en)); avoid |

The recommended setup is PostHog's EU service as the main tool, configured so that by default it keeps no lasting identifier on the device. On top of that comes a one-time, plainly worded opt-in ("Help improve TwinClimate: count my visits over time") that allows an anonymous ID for cohort charts. Pure cookieless counters such as Plausible change their hashes daily. They can count visits but cannot follow a cohort across weeks. Two honesty points belong in any pitch. Users who opt in are probably keener than average, so cohort charts will flatter the product. And users who clear their browser come back as "new". Signing up for PostHog means opening a new account, while the brief says "no API keys or accounts unless unavoidable". Proving usage makes it unavoidable, but the decision and the sign-up are the founder's.

### For Decathlon, the data is worth more than the commission

Decathlon Netherlands runs its affiliate programme (where a website earns commission on sales it refers) through **Odyssey Partnerships**, an Amsterdam network for which Decathlon was the first customer. Decathlon's page advertises up to **7% commission** and a 30-day tracking cookie. Third-party listings say 3.6–6%, and the page may be out of date ([Decathlon.nl](https://www.decathlon.nl/c/lp/het-affiliate-programma_5d86498f-9d86-44eb-897f-2b7cf665ee05); [Frankwatching](https://www.frankwatching.com/channel/odyssey-lanceert-odyssey-partnerships-en-lost-de-incrementality-uitdaging-op-voor-de-affiliate-industrie-decathlon-is-de-launching-customer/); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/decathlon-nl-affiliate-program/)). Affiliate clicks on sports products turn into sales roughly 0.5–4% of the time ([FloatingCTA](https://floatingcta.com/insights/affiliate-marketing-conversion-rate/)). Here is our rough estimate. If 5–15% of monthly users click a shop link and 1–3% of those clicks become a €40–80 order, commission comes to about **€1–25 per 1,000 monthly users per month**. That is not a business model at demo scale. What a brand values is the signal: which needs appear in which weather, how often a suggestion backed by a reason leads to a click, and how many users already own an item. Odyssey credits publishers by how much extra they actually cause to be sold, so it may credit a "you need this today" suggestion more fairly than networks that only reward the last click. That is an inference worth raising with Decathlon. Real affiliate links are a V3 item, so joining is a founder decision. Later on, joining would give hard sales numbers that no counter on a phone can.

Decathlon is more likely to become a commercial partner than an investor. Its closest precedent is a **five-year deal with Freeletics**, announced in April 2025, that bundles app coaching with Decathlon equipment. Decathlon's stated reason was that about 70% of its fitness-equipment users don't know how to use it properly, and about half drop off within three months ([Decathlon UK](https://decathlon-uk.prezly.com/decathlon-and-freeletics-revolutionize-fitness-training)). TwinClimate's matching pitch is weather-precise kit advice that helps customers buy the right item and then use it. Decathlon Pulse, its investment arm since July 2024, mostly buys majority stakes in established firms such as Bikeleasing ([IADS](https://www.iads.org/member/news/decathlon-launches-pulse-for-strategic-diversification); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/news/2025/12/latham-advises-decathlon-pulse-on-acquisition-of-majority-stake-in-bikeleasing-group)), so it is not a realistic early funder. Its "Supported by Decathlon" programme runs three-month trial projects but asks for a registered company, **at least two full-time founders** and traction ([Just Retail](https://www.justretail.news/noticias/retail-supported-by-decathlon-startups-sportstech/)). Its first round picked 4 of 114 start-ups ([Alimarket](https://www.alimarket.es/nonfood/noticia/336421/decathlon-pone-en-marcha-la-estrategia-de-innovacion-digital-decathlon-open-innovation)). Decathlon also runs its own Decathlon Coach app ([Decathlon Coach](https://www.decathloncoach.com/en/home/advices/advice/app-decathlon-coach1)), so it could add kit advice itself. Better first steps for an Amsterdam founder are Decathlon Netherlands' online-marketing team and local store managers (for example, a QR code at the running wall). Dutch sport-innovation schemes are another route. Sportinnovator offers innovation vouchers of **€5,000–25,000** ([Sportinnovator](https://www.sportinnovator.nl/english/)), and SportUp Boost NL runs a three-month early-stage programme linked to Innovatielab Thialf and the Dutch Sport Tech Fund ([Sport & Strategie](https://www.sportenstrategie.nl/sporteconomie/sportup-boost-nl-versterkt-sportinnovatiekracht-met-nieuwe-samenwerking/)). Whether a solo founder without a company can apply was not checked.

### Later, after the first milestone

Several bigger steps follow once the first milestone is reached. German translation would widen the market. Real affiliate links through Odyssey (V3) would produce actual sales data for Decathlon. A co-founder would be needed before any pre-seed round or Decathlon's own programme. An optional account would stop comfort profiles from being lost when people change device, which needs a server. A Garmin watch widget (Connect IQ) could follow, and route suggestions (V2) after that. Each of these needs the founder's explicit go-ahead under the brief.

### The first milestone to take to Decathlon

Aim for the end of February 2027, about 16 weeks after analytics go live. The targets: at least **1,000 people in the Netherlands who have made a kit list**; **300–500 weekly active users**; **25% or more of each weekly cohort still active in week 4**, with the curve flattening; WAU/MAU of 50% or more; **5–7% weekly growth for eight weeks in a row**, at least 70% from unpaid sources; feedback on at least 30% of finished sessions; a measured shop funnel; and named pilots with two or three clubs or one race organiser, plus five to ten quotes from users. "Active in week 4" is a looser measure than strict day-30 retention. If an investor asks for day-30 retention, give the strict figure and expect it to be lower. Beating the 3–4% fitness baseline by a wide margin, say 10%, is the claim to aim for. No source sets a threshold for partner talks, so this milestone is a judgement built from the frameworks and channel estimates above. It is enough to open a commercial conversation with Decathlon Netherlands and to apply for Dutch sport-innovation support. An investment round needs more: a co-founder, several months of flattening cohort curves and, as a rough judgement, low thousands of weekly users.

### Risks that could sink the plan

| Risk | Why it is real | How to blunt it |
|---|---|---|
| Big Dutch weather apps add kit advice, or users find the phone's built-in weather "good enough" | Buienradar and Infoplaza reach 10–16 million people a month; free alternatives are a main reason people leave apps | Lead with the kit answer, its reasons and race matching, not the forecast; be the fastest route to an answer |
| Decathlon or another brand builds its own tool | Assos and On/Satisfy/Bandit built their own; Decathlon already runs Decathlon Coach | Pitch what a brand can't easily copy: learned comfort data, race matching, neutral advice and weather-tagged demand data |
| Platform rules (Strava, Garmin) | Strava's 10-athlete cap, developer subscription and AI ban, and the Relive cut-off; Garmin's programme is paused | Keep integrations optional; own the sharing loop through links and cards |
| Limits of a solo founder | Decathlon's programme needs two founders; investors judge the team; outreach eats hours | Protect 5–10 hours a week for outreach; look for a co-founder among club contacts; accept a partner-first path |
| Shaky measurement or legal grey areas | Opt-in users skew keen; cleared browsers look like new users; regulators say "cookieless" is not automatically exempt | Explain the method openly; count without cookies by default; publish a privacy notice; get an adviser check |
| Seasons and calm weather | Weather-app use falls in calm spells; fitness installs drop about 20% by April and 44% by May after the January peak ([Digital Yield Group](https://digitalyieldgroup.com/blog/health-fitness-apps-the-resolutioner-churn-problem/)) | Expect dips; use spring and summer races as second peaks; show cohorts by season |
| App-store hurdles | Google needs a phone and 12 testers; iPhone web push needs a Home Screen install first | Recruit testers from clubs; start with the store that fits the founder's phone |
| Open-Meteo limits and terms | The free tier is limited per internet address per day, and cloud sessions hit it; its terms for commercial use were not researched | Build race pages as cached static files; check Open-Meteo's terms before any affiliate income or partner deal |
| Features are easy to copy | Do I Need A Coat? already learns from feedback | Win on speed in the niche, race matching, and Dutch places and races |

## Conclusion

"How do we get as many users as possible?" turns out to be the wrong question for this stage. What persuades both investors and brands is a small, defined group that keeps coming back and whose behaviour reveals demand: which kit people need in which weather, and whether a suggestion with a reason leads to a click. TwinClimate already produces exactly that signal, but it counts it on each phone instead of in one place. Fixing that takes about a week. Every week of delay is a week of cohort history that can never be recovered, and cohort history is the asset investors look at first.

The other shift is about where the effort now goes. The software is largely built. What limits growth now is the founder's time in clubs, at races and in conversations, plus a handful of decisions the brief still postpones: reminders, Dutch, a small server, a co-founder. Race matching is the hook for press, organisers and investors, but the everyday kit list is what has to earn the weekly habit. If the coming winter shows a flattening cohort curve among Amsterdam runners, TwinClimate can claim something that neither the Dutch weather giants nor the many small what-to-wear tools have publicly shown: athletes come back to it because it has learned what they need.
