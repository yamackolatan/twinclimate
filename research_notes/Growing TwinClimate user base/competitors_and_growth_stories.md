## 1. Strava, komoot, Runna, Garmin Connect, Zwift (+ Relive as a Dutch analogue): early growth drivers, user numbers, what made them viral or sticky

### Takeaway
None of these apps grew through ads early on. Strava started with one passionate niche (US road cyclists), handed hardware to a few beta testers, and built segments and leaderboards that turned every group ride into word of mouth. Runna began as hand-made PDF plans and grew through referrals and relatable creators following a plan from week 1 to race day. komoot grew on community route content plus cheap one-off region unlocks. Relive (Rotterdam) grew by riding on Strava: its output was a shareable video. Large numbers (Strava 180–200M, komoot 45M, Relive 20M+) took 10–15 years, and the 2024–2025 period brought platform lock-down (Strava API limits) and paywalls (komoot), which hurt small apps that depend on them.

### Cited Findings

**Strava: history (2008–2011)**
- The founders chose "passionate road cyclists" as their first market, to build fierce loyalty before adding other sports (Stanford eCorner transcript) — [Stanford STVP transcript](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/strava-finds-its-niche-transcript.pdf)
- They sent **ten Garmin head units** to passionate cyclists on the US West and East Coasts as first beta users, then ran **East Coast vs West Coast competitions during the 2008 Tour de France** to drive feedback and usage — [Techstadium growth-playbook analysis](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook)
- Segments were built because cyclists said climbs mattered most. Leaderboards followed because riders wanted to compare themselves with friends on the same segment (Mark Gainey, podcast summary) — [Terra podcast with Mark Gainey](https://landing-cf.tryterra.co/podcast/podcast-with-mark-gainey-co-founder-of-strava-97bfd4bd85e9). Co-founder Davis Kitchel based segment leaderboards on an unofficial hill-climb competition from a late-1990s group ride — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/reports/strava)
- Group rides give cyclists many shared training moments, which made early word of mouth easy — [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook). Word of mouth is described as the main growth driver once Strava caught on among cyclists (low-authority blog) — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/strava-doing-things-that-dont-scale)
- Strava launched in 2009. The original website took Garmin uploads, the first mobile app came in 2011, and the free tier at first allowed only 5 uploads a month — [Wikipedia: Strava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strava)
- Any user can create a segment, and it doesn't have to be uphill. That gives a self-generating supply of local competitions — [BikeTips](https://biketips.com/what-is-a-strava-kom/)

**Strava: scale and business (2020–2026)**
- About 50M users and 3 billion activities in 2020 (secondary guide) — [BikeTips](https://biketips.com/what-is-a-strava-kom/). "Over 120 million" users by Oct 2024, with growth among Gen Z and millennials as run clubs took off after the pandemic — [Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2024/10/19/strava-profile/)
- Dec 2025 Year in Sport release: **"over 180 million users across more than 185 countries"**. Running is the most popular sport, the number of clubs almost quadrupled in 2025 to ~1 million, and Gen Z is the fastest-growing group — [Strava press release via Adnkronos](https://www.adnkronos.com/immediapress/eng/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-revealing-that-doomscrolling-is-out-movement-is-in_iPmltuOBI6UULjUCMcmDr). 14 billion kudos given in 2025, +20% on 2024 — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025)
- May 2025: valuation of **$2.2B** in a round led by Sequoia. The WSJ reported >150M users and close to **$500M ARR**. Strava says it passed **200M users** by Aug 2026. In **Feb 2026 Strava confidentially filed a draft S-1** for a US IPO, but no concrete timeline has been set — [Forge Global](https://forgeglobal.com/insights/strava-upcoming-ipo-news/); [the5krunner on the IPO filing](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/09/strava-ipo-filing-3-billion-valuation-analysis/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/strava/). Subscriber count has not been disclosed.
- **Platform lock-down:** from 11 Nov 2024 Strava's API agreement lets third-party apps show a user's Strava data only to that user. It bans using API data in AI models and protects Strava's "look and feel". Reports said this hit apps such as VeloViewer and Intervals.icu — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-api-changes/); [TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/strava-api-agreement-update/98191)

**Runna**
- Origin: in June 2021 Dom Maskell (ex-McKinsey) coached his friend Ben Parker for his first marathon. Runna started as personalised **PDF** plans before becoming an app — [Welltodo founder Q&A](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/); [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/news/runna-acquired-by-strava/)
- Within about a year of launch, "hundreds of thousands" of users signed up in 180+ countries — [Runna careers page](https://runna.notion.site/Careers-at-Runna-8d9ba4280e70489aad1fad2b1a2aaf47); [McKinsey alumni / Forbes 30u30 2024](https://www.mckinsey.com/alumni/news-and-events/global-news/alumni-news/2024-05-meet-the-4-alumni-on-forbes-30-under-30-europe-list-for-2024)
- Acquisition channels named by the founders: **organic (especially referrals)**, paid performance marketing, influencers and partnerships. They say "if you invest in building an exceptional product, team and community, then the users will come" — [Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/)
- Brand partnerships with reality-TV stars, fitness brands and Olympians are credited with driving its rise — [Startups.co.uk](https://startups.co.uk/news/runna-acquired-by-strava/)
- Creator strategy (one commentator's analysis): Runna picked **relatable creators over elite athletes**, and their content follows the plan's week-by-week arc to race day, which mirrors the product promise — [Growing Viral newsletter](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/runna-choosing-relatability-over-celebrities)
- "All over Instagram, TikTok and Strava". **48,000+ App Store ratings at 4.9★**, recommended unprompted on running subreddits and RunTok — [Believe in the Run](https://believeintherun.com/strava-runna/)
- £5M round led by JamJar in Nov 2023, >£8M raised in total, 120+ staff in London and Boston — [Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/)
- **Strava bought Runna in April 2025** (terms undisclosed). At the time Runna cost $119.99/yr vs Strava $79.99/yr — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/strava-acquires-runna-amid-spike-in-race-day-goals/); [Advnture](https://www.advnture.com/tech/apps-software/a-perfect-fit-stravas-150-million-users-get-a-new-running-coach-as-brand-acquires-coaching-app-runna). A Strava + Runna bundle launched in 2025 at **$149.99/yr (US)**, annual only, with a 30-day trial — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-and-runna-launch-joint-subscription/); [Endurance.biz](https://endurance.biz/2025/industry-news/new-strava-runna-combined-subscription-bundle/)

**komoot**
- Founded in Potsdam in 2010, with the public app launch in 2013. It launched in the UK and other European countries in summer 2013 after raising €1M from a German public fund, and reached the US a year later — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1061063) (plus aggregator summaries)
- 40M registered users (June 2024). Route suggestions combine millions of recorded tours with OpenStreetMap data and community photos, tips and "Highlights" — [komoot newsroom](https://newsroom.komoot.com/238612-komoot-breaks-the-40-million-mark-for-registered-users)
- Old monetisation: one-off unlocks, about **£3.99 per region, £8.99 per region bundle, £29.99 for the world** — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/new-komoot-users-send-to-devices)
- **Bending Spoons (owner of Evernote and WeTransfer) bought komoot in March 2025** at **45M users**. The CEO said the founding team's approach had "reached its limit" — [Bending Spoons press release via Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/bending-spoons-acquires-komoot); mass layoffs were expected, with ~150 staff in doubt — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/komoot-acquisition)
- From **27 Feb 2025** new users need Premium (~$59.99/yr) to send routes to devices, and region packs are no longer sold to new accounts. Critics called this "we don't want any new customers" — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/03/11/komoot-confirms-we-dont-want-any-new-customers-in-ridiculous-pricing-move/); [DC Rainmaker](https://dcrainmaker.com/2025/03/komoots-expanded-paywalls-trying-to-make-sense-of-it.html)

**Garmin Connect**
- Garmin's FY2025 fitness revenue rose 33% to **$2.36B**, with operating income of $726M, driven by wearables — [Garmin Q4/FY2025 results via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/313955/garmin-announces-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2025-results); [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/financial/garmin-posts-record-revenue-in-2025-driven-by-fitness/119646.article)
- The widely quoted "~35M Garmin Connect users" dates from 2021–2022 and has not been updated, so no current official figure exists — [the5krunner, May 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/05/01/garmin-connect-users-2026/)
- Connect's growth comes from hardware: every watch sold creates a Connect user. A solo app can't copy that, but it can plug into it (see Q4: Garmin's heat-acclimation feature).

**Zwift (brief)**
- Grew strongly in COVID lockdowns when gyms closed, then slowed as riders went back outdoors — [the5krunner, Jan 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/23/peak-zwift-2026-indoor-cycling-trends/)
- Subscribers: "1M+ active subscribers in 2024" (claimed), vs DC Rainmaker's estimate of 750–900k. The monthly price rose from $14.99 to $19.99 in May 2024. Zwift cut 15% of staff in March 2023 and cut again in February 2024, when the co-CEO left ("Growth has not rebounded at a fast enough pace…"). Free rival MyWhoosh is growing — [the5krunner 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/23/peak-zwift-2026-indoor-cycling-trends/); [Escape Collective](https://escapecollective.com/further-layoffs-at-zwift-as-co-ceo-walks-away/); [the5krunner 2024](https://the5krunner.com/2024/02/06/zwift-ceo-resigns-more-layoffs)

**Relive (Dutch, Rotterdam): the closest home-market analogue**
- Founded by three Dutch friends after a 2016 cycling trip to Tenerife. They logged rides on Strava but missed the visual side, so Relive turns GPS data from Strava/Garmin plus photos into a 3D video — [Esri ArcNews](https://www.esri.com/about/newsroom/arcnews/with-3d-maps-athletes-relive-their-global-adventures)
- May 2017: almost 500k sign-ups, ~100,000 videos a day, $1M seed round — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/relive). 1.2M users with a 7-person team (Winter 2018) — [Esri](https://www.esri.com/about/newsroom/arcnews/with-3d-maps-athletes-relive-their-global-adventures)
- Later "over 20 million users and more than one billion activities", reported when Relive bought Amsterdam's JOIN cycling-training app (article undated in results) — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/relive-acquires-cycling-training-app-join/). Total funding reported at only ~€1.2M — [StartupBlink](https://startupblink.com/startup/relive--2)

### Inferences
- The pattern behind the big winners: **one tight niche → a shareable or competitive output → organic spread through existing groups** (group rides, run clubs, Strava feeds), with monetisation coming later. Strava took ~16 years to reach 180M+. komoot needed ~12 years for 45M. Investors will judge a demo-stage app on engagement and growth rate in a niche, not on absolute numbers.
- Runna and Relive are the most relevant models for a solo or small EU team. Runna started with a manual service (PDFs) and a goal-driven product (race day). Relive got to 20M+ users on ~€1.2M of funding by riding on Strava's distribution with a shareable artefact. TwinClimate has a race-date goal like Runna and could make shareable artefacts like Relive.
- Relying on another platform is risky. Strava's Nov 2024 API limits and komoot's 2025 paywall show that platforms change terms. Any Strava integration should be an extra growth channel, not the core product.
- Runna's "relatable creator follows the plan to race day" format fits TwinClimate's race-matching story well (for example, "training in Kalmar-like conditions from Amsterdam" as an 8-week series).

### Gaps
- No primary-source detail on when KOM crowns launched or how much they drove growth. The "~99% organic" Strava growth claim appears only in a podcast-page summary (unverified).
- Runna's revenue growth (a "238% in 2023" claim) and early paid subscribers (claims of 7,000 and 10,000) appear only in unverified blogs that conflict with each other. The launch year is also given as both 2021 and 2022. Runna's current (2026) subscriber numbers are not public.
- komoot revenue (one secondary report: €36M, profitable) and post-acquisition layoffs (one report: up to 85% of ~150 staff, citing BikeRadar Sept 2025) are unverified.
- Strava's subscriber count and conversion rate are not disclosed. Estimates of 3–15% of users are unreliable.
- Relive's current (2024–2026) user count and funding are not confirmed.

## 2. Athlete-relevant weather apps in NL/EU: user numbers, business models, what athletes like

### Takeaway
The Dutch general-weather market is saturated and owned by two media/data groups: **Infoplaza** (Weeronline, Weerplaza, Buienalarm, ~16M monthly users combined) and **RTL** (Buienradar, ~10M monthly reach). Both run on ads plus cheap ad-free upgrades. Windy (Czech) shows that an enthusiast-grade, ad-free map tool can reach tens of millions a month worldwide. Cycling-specific route-weather tools (Epic Ride Weather, myWindsock) are small, loved niche products with low subscription prices. None of them tells athletes what to wear or matches race climate.

### Cited Findings

**Buienradar (RTL)**
- RTL bought the Buienradar website and apps in 2011. It began as a moving rain map and grew into a full weather platform. Downloads passed 5.5M on Play Store + App Store (2018 figure). An ad-free "Plus" subscription launched in 2020 at ~€3.49/yr (2021 figure). The founders sold with a 3-year non-compete and later launched Weerslag (2014) — Dutch tech press summarised in search: [AndroidWorld](https://androidworld.nl/nieuws/buienradar-wordt-beter-in-het-buitenland/); [iPhoned – Weerslag](https://www.iphoned.nl/apps/weerslag/)
- Monthly reach of **~10 million people** across TV, web, app and social (factsheet, about 2023) — [Ad Alliance Buienradar factsheet](https://adalliance.nl/wp-content/uploads/Factsheet-Buienradar.pdf)
- RTL stopped giving third-party developers free Buienradar data, and some apps disappeared from the App Store. Weerplaza then offered its own radar data to those developers — [iCulture](https://www.iculture.nl/apps/regen-verdwijnt-uit-app-store-vanwege-nieuw-beleid-buienradar/); [iCulture – Weerplaza](https://www.iculture.nl/apps/weerplaza-geeft-buienkaarten-aan-iphone-apps-die-buienradar-terugtrekt/)
- Dutch roundups name Buienradar the default first choice (rain radar, wind maps, rain alerts; ads in the free version) — [iCreate](https://www.icreatemagazine.nl/koopadvies/beste-weer-app/); [AndroidPlanet](https://www.androidplanet.nl/apps/beste-weer-apps/)

**Infoplaza: Weeronline, Weerplaza, Buienalarm**
- **Buienalarm passed 5 million active users in June 2025**, and use rose even in an unusually dry half-year (50% fewer rain hours than average). Infoplaza's platforms together reach **~16M users a month** — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/buienalarm-passeert-5-miljoen-gebruikers-extreem-droog-jaar)
- Infoplaza bought Buienalarm from its creator Franklin van Velthuizen — [iCulture](https://www.iculture.nl/nieuws/buienalarm-infoplaza-overname/). Infoplaza also bought Weeronline — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/weeronline-handen-infoplaza)
- Weeronline was voted best weather website of 2025 (company wire) — [Emerce Wire](https://www.emerce.nl/wire/weeronline-verkozen-tot-beste-weerwebsite-2025). Reviewers call it well designed and highly customisable, with ads unless you pay for Plus — [iCreate](https://www.icreatemagazine.nl/koopadvies/beste-weer-app/)
- Ahrefs traffic estimates (modelled, June 2025): weeronline.nl ~6.4M and buienradar.nl ~6.1M visits a month from search — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/top/weeronline.nl)

**Windy.com (Czech)**
- Founded Nov 2014 by Ivo Lukačovič (founder of Seznam) after existing forecasts kept letting him down on snow-kiting trips — [Wikipedia: Windy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windy_(weather_service)); [Bloomberg 2020](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-02-06/windy-com-is-the-new-favorite-website-for-the-climate-obsessed)
- "Tens of millions" of people use it each month. Service revenue grew from about 50M to more than 400M CZK in five years. Windy later bought Swiss Meteoblue, and Lukačovič turned down a Microsoft acquisition offer — [CzechCrunch](https://cc.cz/z-50-na-400-milionu-za-pet-let-aplikace-na-pocasi-windy-iva-lukacovice-dal-roste-a-naramne-vydelava/); [CzechCrunch – Meteoblue](https://cc.cz/ivo-lukacovic-se-svycary-inspiroval-ted-je-kupuje-meteoblue-prechazi-pod-windy/); [CzechCrunch – Microsoft](https://cc.cz/microsoft-chtel-koupit-windy-com-zakladatele-seznamu-dost-je-sokovalo-ze-jsem-odmitl-rika-ivo-lukacovic/)
- Semrush estimates 39.5M visits in Dec 2025, down from 59.4M in Oct 2025 (seasonal) — [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/windy.com/overview/). Publicly committed to staying free and ad-free (premium tier for more detail) — [Windy Community](https://community.windy.com/topic/11841/in-these-challenging-times-windy-com-will-stay-free-and-without-advertisement/63)
- Dutch cyclists mention Windy for its ~50 map layers, though some find the subscription too pricey for a hobby (forum opinions summarised in a Dutch roundup) — [Wielerflits](https://www.wielerflits.nl/nieuws/handige-apps-voor-wielrenners/)
- Note: **Windy.app** is a different company (founded 2015, ~45 staff per PitchBook). It offers "routes with weather", including import of Strava routes — [Windy.app](https://windy.app/features/routes-with-weather.html); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/169075-09)

**Ventusky (InMeteo, Czech)**
- "Used by millions worldwide" (undated Czech business article). No reliable monthly figures were found — [BusinessInfo.cz](https://www.businessinfo.cz/clanky/pestrobarevne-meteomapy-s-presnymi-daty-svet-si-oblibil-aplikaci-ceske-firmy/)

**Cycling route-weather tools**
- **Epic Ride Weather:** built in 2016 by David Green on Dark Sky data "for fun on his long commute". Used by Jumbo-Visma since 2018. Gives a forecast every 10 minutes of ride time along a route from Strava, Ride with GPS, Garmin, komoot or Plotaroute — [Velo review](https://velo.outsideonline.com/gear/tech-wearables/epic-ride-weather-review-one-of-the-handiest-cycling-apps-just-got-better/?scope=anon); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1159726753). Pricing: free trial, then $2.99/month or $14.99/year (2022) — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2022/08/11/strava-epic-ride-weather). Rated **4.9★ from 739 reviews** (US App Store, March 2026), so a small user base — [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/1159726753). Called "the best cycling-specific weather app we've used" — [Wrench Science](https://wrenchscience.com/blogs/news/the-best-apps-for-cyclists)
- **myWindsock:** a web tool that imports routes from Strava, RWGPS or GPX/TCX. A "Virtual Athlete" rides the course for pacing, with W′ balance based on the critical-power model. Also analyses past rides — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/mywindsock-takes-strava-nerdiness-to-the-next-level/); [myWindsock](https://mywindsock.com/page/?p=3350). It markets "race day optimisation" — [myWindsock race-day guide](https://mywindsock.com/page/?p=3165). Its weather-impact data appears on VeloViewer segment pages, distribution through partner placement — [search summary of myWindsock/VeloViewer](https://mywindsock.com/page/?p=2165)
- Others: **Brezza** (iOS "Cycling Weather & Prep", imports Strava/RWGPS routes) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6740043033). **Strautomator** (route forecast for Strava club events, PRO feature) — [Strautomator docs](https://docs.strautomator.com/features/upcoming-events-map)

### Inferences
- Competing with Buienradar or Weeronline on general weather is pointless. They own NL distribution through TV, press and SEO. TwinClimate should be positioned as the **"athlete layer"** that answers "what do I wear at 08:00 in the Amsterdamse Bos", which none of them do.
- Buienalarm passing 5M users in a dry year suggests weather apps grow through **habit and alerts tied to a decision** (cycle now or wait?). TwinClimate's version is the session-time alert ("your 08:00 run: arm warmers").
- Dutch willingness to pay is low (Buienradar Plus ~€3.49/yr, Epic Ride Weather $14.99/yr). Keeping the core free, with the value going to brands, fits the market.
- Buienradar cutting off third-party data shows the same platform risk as Strava. TwinClimate's choice of Open-Meteo (open, keyless) avoids that dependency.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 unique-user figure for Weeronline alone or for the Buienradar app alone. Available figures mix reach, visits and downloads.
- No NL-specific user numbers for Windy, Ventusky, Epic Ride Weather or myWindsock.
- No source found for "Weather4Cycling" (the name may be wrong). A Dutch roundup claimed komoot shows "how to dress for the weather" on tours, but I couldn't attribute or verify this, so it needs checking.

## 3. "What to wear" running/cycling tools: popularity, distribution, gaps TwinClimate fills

### Takeaway
"What to wear" tools are plentiful but small and fragmented. Most are single-developer iOS apps or simple web calculators that use current or near-term conditions, a generic "run hot/cold" slider and whole-outfit suggestions. A few brands (Assos; On + Satisfy + Bandit) have built their own as a sales tool. One new iOS app ("Do I Need A Coat?") already learns from too-cold/too-warm feedback. No tool found combines exact-spot, session-hour forecasts with a learned per-body-part profile, item-level reasons, a packing checklist and race-climate matching. No standalone what-to-wear tool publishes large user numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **Runner's World "What to Wear" / "What Should I Wear?":** recommends an outfit (including e.g. whether to wear a hat) from temperature, wind, sun or rain, and how you like to feel. It runs as a web tool on a large publisher's site, so it gets SEO and brand traffic. The description comes only from weak secondary pages — [secondary summary](https://atendimento.ultralims.com.br/quick-answer-runners-world-what/)
- **dressmyrun.com:** a free web tool that geolocates you, reads current conditions and suggests an outfit, with a run-hot/run-cold slider. It **doesn't forecast ahead** or alert you when conditions change (description from a competitor's blog) — [NorthLab Apps blog](https://northlabapps.com/blog/what-to-wear-running.md)
- **What to Wear Cycling** (Christopher Mullen, iOS, £1.99 at review time): forecast → head-to-toe kit list, a hot/cold slider, and a **"Tell the Peloton" button that shares the kit list with your group** — [London Cyclist](https://londoncyclist.co.uk/new-app-what-to-wear); [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/tech-news/226746-cycling-app-week-what-wear-cycling)
- **Cycling Gear** (iOS): what to wear now, in 2 hours or in 4 hours, from temperature, wind and rain — [iPhoneAddict](https://iphoneaddict.fr/apps/sports/cycling-gear.html). **Velo Wear:** location or manual conditions → layers, materials and accessories — [Patrick Steinert](https://www.patricksteinert.de/velo-wear/)
- **Do I Need A Coat?** (iOS, running/cycling/walking): uses Apple WeatherKit and **learns from your "too cold / too warm" feedback** to personalise over time. This is the closest analogue to TwinClimate's comfort loop — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6751337989)
- **Running Weather: What to Wear** (iOS): free with no subscription, outfit suggestions from weather plus personal preferences — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/hn/app/running-weather-what-to-wear/id6474908980). **RunnersKit:** a runner figure dressed for current conditions, with a Pro tier that plans runs up to 11 hours ahead — [listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/runnerskit-run-weather/6775235739). **RunDida:** a web guide by temperature with garments per body zone — [RunDida](https://rundida.com/tools/clothing-temp-guide/)
- **Brand-built tools:** **Assos** (cycling apparel) launched an app that reads the local forecast (temperature, rain, wind) and recommends the right Assos kit for that day — [BikeBiz](https://bikebiz.com/cycling-apparel-brand-assos-launches-its-first-mobile-app/). **whatdoiwear.run**, a collaboration between Satisfy, On and Bandit, uses exact location, time of day, temperature, wind, humidity, UV, rain and run type ("LONG"/"SPEED") to recommend specific branded items with technical specs (directory listings, Feb 2026; unverified) — [ProductCool](https://www.productcool.com/product/whatdoiwear-run); [FeedBagel](https://feedbagel.com/post/whatdoiwearrun-weather-based-outfit-recommendation-engine-for-runners)
- **Strava-connected weather tools:** **Klimat** automatically writes the weather (temperature, rain, humidity, wind) into each Strava activity's description. The free tier adds Klimat branding, which the paid tier (~$5/yr or ~$1/month in 2019 reviews) removes — [Bikerumor](https://bikerumor.com/2019/01/07/hows-the-weather-klimat-app-bundles-ride-conditions-into-your-strava-feed/); [Aerogeeks](https://aerogeeks.com/2019/08/05/klimat-review/); [road.cc](https://road.cc/index.php/tech_news/251070-cycling-app-week-klimat/). As of 2025 there was no sign that **Strava itself** had added a native route weather forecast; third parties fill that gap — [search summary incl. Bike-Magazin](https://www.bike-magazin.de/komponenten/fahrradcomputer/news-von-strava-uebernahme-von-trainings-app-und-neue-features/)
- **Common rule of thumb** found in these tools: dress for 15–20°F (~8–11°C) warmer than the actual temperature, and feeling slightly chilly for the first 5 minutes means you're dressed right — [NorthLab Apps blog](https://northlabapps.com/blog/what-to-wear-running.md)

### Inferences
- The space shows proven **user need but no scaled winner**. No standalone what-to-wear tool reports large user numbers, and the most-used version is probably a feature on a big publisher's site (Runner's World). So "what to wear" on its own probably won't create very large numbers. It needs a distribution host (SEO, a community, a brand or a platform).
- Brands (Assos; On/Satisfy/Bandit) building their own versions shows that **brands value "what to wear" as a sales entry point**. That supports the Decathlon pitch, but it also means a large retailer could build its own. TwinClimate's case to a brand should rest on the learned comfort data, the race matching and the neutral "advice first" stance, not on the basic temperature-to-outfit table.
- What TwinClimate does that the competitors above don't (as far as their descriptions show): exact-coordinate, session-window hourly forecasts; five body parts with a reason per item; a learned, temperature-banded profile per sport; a packing checklist; typical weather beyond the 16-day forecast; and race-climate matching. "Do I Need A Coat?" overlaps on learning from feedback, so the learning loop alone is not unique.
- Two cheap viral mechanics already in this space: **What to Wear Cycling's "Tell the Peloton"** (share the kit list with your group) and **Klimat's branded line in every Strava activity**. Both could be adapted, for example as a shareable kit card for run-club WhatsApp groups.

### Gaps
- No install counts or traffic figures were found for any what-to-wear tool, including the Runner's World tool (its own page couldn't be fetched or confirmed).
- No current data on whether Garmin, Coros or Apple Weather give clothing advice. I found nothing showing that they do; Garmin's related feature is heat acclimation (see Q4).
- Klimat's user count and whether it still works under Strava's Nov 2024 API terms were not confirmed.
- I couldn't confirm whether Decathlon has its own what-to-wear feature in its apps (e.g., Decathlon Coach).

## 4. Race-climate / heat-acclimatisation tools: is there a direct competitor for "train in race-like conditions"?

### Takeaway
I found **no direct competitor** that takes a target race's historical race-hour climate and finds nearby training places and times that match it. What exists: per-race historical weather pages (FindMyMarathon, PJAMM for Ironman bike courses), general climate-average sites (Weather Spark), course-specific pacing and wind tools (myWindsock), and watch-based heat and altitude acclimation scores (Garmin). These are pieces TwinClimate combines, and they also point to SEO and integration angles.

### Cited Findings
- **FindMyMarathon** race weather pages give, per race, race-morning weather averages over ~12–18 years (start temperature, humidity, wind), a "PR Score" and "Course Score", and links to pace-band products — [FindMyMarathon – Miami example](https://www.findmymarathon.com/weather-detail.php?zname=Miami+Marathon); [Maine example](https://www.findmymarathon.com/weather-detail.php?zname=Maine+Marathon)
- **PJAMM Cycling** Ironman course pages send athletes to a "PJAMM Cycling Weather Tool" for current and historic weather. Whether it covers heat stress wasn't confirmed — [PJAMM – IRONMAN Maryland](https://pjammcycling.com/triathlon/22.IRONMAN-Maryland-Bike-Course-and-Run-Profile)
- **Weather Spark:** typical weather by month, day and even hour for **145,449 locations** worldwide. It won't predict, but historic trends give a "best guess" — [Travel Massive listing](https://travelmassive.com/posts/weather-spark-844438344); [FlowingData 2011](https://flowingdata.com/2011/03/14/weatherspark-for-more-graphs-about-the-weather-than-you-will-ever-need/); [Kevin Kelly Cool Tools](https://kk.org/cooltools/weatherspark/)
- **Garmin Heat & Altitude Acclimation** (Training Status): outdoor GPS activities above **22°C** (or above 800 m altitude) count toward a 0–100% acclimation score. The score needs phone weather data, takes at least ~4 training days to reach full acclimation, and fades after a few days without heat (forum claim) — [Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-3B607AEB-9EB5-4BF3-A38D-1F9926BB9F65/EN-US/GUID-70386BCC-5682-4C5C-9A87-C32AF9B6473B.html); [We Love Cycling](https://www.welovecycling.com/wide/2022/11/10/how-does-garmin-measure-your-heat-altitude-acclimation/)
- **myWindsock** "race day optimisation": a virtual athlete rides the race course in forecast conditions for pacing — [myWindsock](https://mywindsock.com/page/?p=3165)
- What athletes do today: forum advice is to pull location averages and records from general sites like weather.com for race sites, while noting these are only trends — [Slowtwitch forum](https://forum.slowtwitch.com/forum/Slowtwitch_Forums_C1/Triathlon_Forum_F1/coldest_ironman%3F_P473060/). Static one-off climatologies exist, for example Cornell NRCC's Boston Marathon race-day climatology (2018) — [Cornell NRCC](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/services/blog/2018/04/11/index.html)
- A search for a tool comparing training conditions with race-day climate found none. The search summary concluded that athletes would have to pull archives themselves — [search-result set incl. FindMyMarathon/NRCC](https://www.findmymarathon.com/weather-detail.php?zname=Texas+Marathon)

### Inferences
- "Train in race-like conditions within my travel range" appears to be **an unoccupied niche**. That is a strong investor story, but the market is small: mostly triathletes and marathoners with a far-away target race.
- FindMyMarathon's per-race pages and Weather Spark's 145k location pages suggest a copyable **programmatic-SEO play**: one public page per popular EU race (e.g., "Ironman Kalmar race-day weather: 10-year typical conditions, what to wear, where to train like it from Amsterdam"). TwinClimate already computes the fingerprint data.
- Garmin's acclimation score uses a crude ">22°C counts" rule. TwinClimate could say "Garmin tracks how acclimated you are; we tell you where and when to train to get there."

### Gaps
- PJAMM's weather tool features and TrainingPeaks' "weather algorithm" (mentioned in a 2016 Kona article) weren't confirmed. Neither seems to do location matching, but that is unverified.
- No traffic figures were found for FindMyMarathon or Weather Spark.
- I did not check whether Ironman's official race pages publish historical weather. Only third-party pages appeared.
- I did not search heat-training products such as CORE body-temperature sensor apps for overlap.

## 5. Lessons: which growth mechanics can a solo founder with no ad budget realistically copy?

### Takeaway
The copyable mechanics are the ones that need effort rather than money: start with one tight community (Strava: road cyclists; Runna: marathon trainees), do unscalable manual work early (Strava's 10 beta testers, Runna's PDFs), make the output shareable inside existing groups (Relive videos, Klimat's Strava line, "Tell the Peloton"), and build SEO pages per race and place (FindMyMarathon, Weather Spark). Paid influencers, reality-TV partnerships (Runna) and hardware bundling (Garmin) are not realistic without capital. Platform integrations help, but they can be withdrawn (Strava API 2024, Buienradar data cut-off).

### Cited Findings
- **Niche first:** Strava targeted passionate road cyclists to build loyalty before other sports — [Stanford STVP](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/strava-finds-its-niche-transcript.pdf)
- **Unscalable early seeding plus a competition hook:** 10 Garmin units to beta users and coast-vs-coast competitions during the 2008 Tour de France — [Techstadium](https://techstadium.substack.com/p/strava-analyzing-the-growth-playbook)
- **Manual MVP:** Runna started as personalised PDF plans for one friend's marathon — [Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/)
- **Referrals and relatable creators beat celebrities:** Runna's founders name organic/referral as a lead channel. Commentary credits relatable creators following the plan's journey — [Welltodo](https://www.welltodoglobal.com/post/ben-parker-and-dom-maskell-co-founders-of-runna/); [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/runna-choosing-relatability-over-celebrities). One general creator playbook suggests recruiting micro-creators (~800–10,000 followers) who already use the product — [Growth Kit Handbook](https://growthkithandbook.notion.site/The-Exact-Strategy-Airbuds-and-Locket-Used-to-Scale-121d142e96d08179a8cbd008b29e17e6)
- **Shareable output on someone else's feed:** Relive turned Strava/Garmin activities into videos and reached ~500k sign-ups in its first year — [TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/relive). Klimat's free tier puts branded weather text in every Strava activity — [Bikerumor](https://bikerumor.com/2019/01/07/hows-the-weather-klimat-app-bundles-ride-conditions-into-your-strava-feed/)
- **Community feature that spreads by itself:** user-created Strava segments and leaderboards — [BikeTips](https://biketips.com/what-is-a-strava-kom/); [Terra podcast](https://landing-cf.tryterra.co/podcast/podcast-with-mark-gainey-co-founder-of-strava-97bfd4bd85e9). Clubs and run clubs are Strava's fastest-growing social unit (~1M clubs, almost 4× in 2025) — [Strava press release](https://www.adnkronos.com/immediapress/eng/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-revealing-that-doomscrolling-is-out-movement-is-in_iPmltuOBI6UULjUCMcmDr)
- **Annual "year in review" shareables:** Strava's Year in Sport report is widely covered by press each December — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025)
- **SEO pages per race:** FindMyMarathon's race-weather pages — [FindMyMarathon](https://www.findmymarathon.com/weather-detail.php?zname=Miami+Marathon). Weather Spark's per-location typical-weather pages — [Travel Massive](https://travelmassive.com/posts/weather-spark-844438344)
- **Founder-led product with no ads:** Windy grew to tens of millions of monthly users while staying ad-free, from a founder solving his own kiting problem (though it was funded by a wealthy founder) — [CzechCrunch](https://cc.cz/nechceme-se-rovnat-pocasi-od-applu-jak-aplikace-windy-iva-lukacovice-vyrostla-do-globalni-jednicky/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-02-06/windy-com-is-the-new-favorite-website-for-the-climate-obsessed)
- **Platform risk:** Strava's Nov 2024 API limits — [Marathon Handbook](https://marathonhandbook.com/strava-api-changes/). Buienradar's data cut-off for third-party apps — [iCulture](https://www.iculture.nl/apps/regen-verdwijnt-uit-app-store-vanwege-nieuw-beleid-buienradar/)
- **Paywalls can stall new-user growth:** komoot's Feb 2025 change was criticised as "we don't want any new customers" — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/03/11/komoot-confirms-we-dont-want-any-new-customers-in-ridiculous-pricing-move/). Zwift's price rise and slowing growth — [the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/01/23/peak-zwift-2026-indoor-cycling-trends/)
- **Weather-event demand:** Buienalarm's use grew even in a dry period, and weather apps peak around events (Windy's traffic swings by season) — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/buienalarm-passeert-5-miljoen-gebruikers-extreem-droog-jaar); [Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/windy.com/overview/)

### Inferences (copyable playbook for TwinClimate, ranked by cost and fit)
1. **Pick one beachhead and own it.** For example, Amsterdam runners training for the TCS Amsterdam Marathon or Dam tot Damloop, or Dutch triathletes with a summer Ironman target. Seed by hand: contact 10–20 Amsterdam run clubs and triathlon clubs, offer a free "race-climate report" for their club's target race, and run it in person (Strava's 10 beta testers, Runna's PDFs).
2. **Shareable kit card / race-climate card** (Relive, Klimat, "Tell the Peloton"): a one-tap image or link ("Saturday 08:00 Vondelpark: feels like 6°, wear: …, made with TwinClimate") for WhatsApp run-club groups and Instagram stories. Group chats are where Dutch run clubs organise.
3. **Programmatic SEO pages per race and per park** (FindMyMarathon / Weather Spark): "Ironman Kalmar weather: 10-year race-hour typical conditions + what to wear + where to train like it from Amsterdam". Caution (inference, not a sourced fact): a Flutter web app renders poorly for search engines, so these pages would probably need to be separate static HTML.
4. **Micro-creators who follow a race block** (Runna): 3–5 Dutch running/triathlon creators with 1k–10k followers documenting "training in Kalmar-like conditions from NL", paid in gear or affiliate share rather than cash.
5. **Seasonal press hooks** (Strava Year in Sport, weather-event spikes): heatwave or storm-day pitches to Dutch press ("what to wear for a run at 30°C"), and a year-end "how Amsterdam runners dressed in 2026" from anonymised feedback data. That data is also a brand-partner asset.
6. **Low-risk integrations:** an optional Strava or Garmin connection to fill in the post-session feedback and weather automatically, while assuming terms can change (Strava 2024, Buienradar). Stay on open data (Open-Meteo).
7. **Don't put up a paywall early** (komoot, Zwift): investors and brands want reach and engagement first. TwinClimate's plan of a free core with brand revenue fits this.
- Not realistically copyable without money: reality-TV and Olympian partnerships, paid performance marketing at scale (Runna), hardware-driven user creation (Garmin), and TV-led reach (Buienradar/RTL).
- Expectation setting: comparable niche tools have small audiences (Epic Ride Weather: 739 US App Store ratings after ~10 years). Very large numbers have come only from social or community platforms (Strava, komoot, Relive) or mass weather brands. A realistic investor milestone is fast growth plus retention in one EU niche, not millions of users.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on what share of growth came from organic vs paid channels for any of these companies (Strava's "~99% organic" is unverified).
- No examples found of a solo founder taking a what-to-wear tool to large scale. The absence of such a case is itself a signal.
- No 2025–2026 data on how many Strava third-party apps survived the API change, or on whether "description-writing" growth (Klimat-style) is still allowed.
- Dutch run-club and triathlon-club counts (to size the beachhead) were not researched here.
